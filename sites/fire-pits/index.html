Why a Fire Pit

The Difference Between a Patio and a Place People Stay

Most backyards have somewhere to sit. Far fewer have a reason to sit there after eight o'clock. A fire feature is the element that changes that — it gives the seating a focal point to face, it makes the space usable on a cool Southern California evening, and it turns a patio into the part of the house people gravitate to.

That is a different job from a pool fire bowl. A fire bowl is architecture: it sits on the pool structure and is admired from across the water. A fire pit or fire table is furniture you gather around — the layout is decided by where the chairs go, not by where the bond beam runs.

Get the height right and the seating arranges itself. Get it wrong and you have a beautiful object nobody sits near.

BellaFina Outdoors is more than a place to buy a fire pit. As an outdoor living design center we help homeowners and trade professionals work through height, size, fuel, ignition and finish, so the feature suits the space it is going into — and so it coordinates with the stone, tile, kitchen and shade structures around it.

Gas Fire Pits

Modern Gas Fire Pits for Patios and Backyards

A gas fire pit lights on demand and goes out the same way. There is no wood to store, no embers drifting toward a neighbour's roof and no ash to shovel out the next morning — which is why most Southern California backyards end up on gas. What varies is how the flame is presented: a wide bed of fire glass reads modern and low, a lava rock bed reads warmer and more textured, and a linear trough suits a long seating run better than a round pit does.

Beneath all of them sits the same set of decisions — burner size, fuel type, ignition and enclosure venting — and all four are easier to settle before anything is built.

  • Round, square and linear burner configurations to suit the seating layout
  • Match-lit, push-button spark or fully electronic ignition with remote options
  • Natural gas or propane, with concealed-tank options on freestanding models
  • Fire glass, lava rock and ceramic log media in a range of finishes

Fire Tables

Outdoor Fire Tables: Dining Height, Bar Height, Lounge Height

A fire table is a fire pit built at a working height, and that one change decides how the space gets used. At lounge height it anchors a set of deep-seating chairs. At dining height it becomes the table people eat at, with the flame running down the middle. At bar height it pairs with stools along the edge of a kitchen run or a balcony rail.

Because the top is a usable surface, material matters more here than on a low pit — concrete, GFRC, porcelain and powder-coated aluminium all wear differently under glasses, sun and coastal air. We can walk you through the trade-offs against the setting the table is going into.

  • Lounge, dining and bar heights to match the furniture around them
  • Rectangular, square and round tops, from small balcony sizes upward
  • Concrete, GFRC, porcelain and powder-coated metal finishes
  • Burner covers and lids so the surface stays usable when the flame is off

How to Choose an Outdoor Fire Pit: What to Decide Before You Order

Five decisions shape how a fire feature looks and how it fits the space — and all five are cheaper to settle before anything is built.

Height & Seating
Lounge height suits deep seating and conversation; dining height turns the feature into the table; bar height works along a rail or a kitchen run. Decide how the space will be used first, because the height decides the furniture and the furniture decides the size.
Built-In or Freestanding
A built-in feature can be veneered to match the hardscape and read as part of the architecture, but it involves an enclosure, venting and a gas line. A freestanding table or bowl arrives finished and can be moved or taken with you. Neither is better; they solve different problems and cost differently.
Fuel Type
Natural gas suits a permanent feature used often, with nothing to refill, but needs a line run to the location. Propane offers flexibility where no line reaches and is frequently concealed in a freestanding base. Wood delivers crackle and smell, with local rules on open burning and air quality to check first. The choice affects burner sizing and the specifications your installer works from.
Burner, Media & Ignition
Burner shape sets the character of the flame — a wide round bed, a square, or a linear trough down a bench run. Fire glass, lava rock and ceramic logs each give a different look. Match-lit is the simplest option; electronic ignition with a remote or wall switch is the convenient one. Match the ignition to how often you expect to use it.
Clearances & Placement
Every product publishes minimum clearances to combustibles and to seating, and an enclosure has venting requirements of its own. Overhead structures, pergolas and shade sails need particular attention. These figures should shape the layout from the start rather than being discovered once the pad is poured.

Fire Pit Questions

Common Questions About Outdoor Fire Pits & Fire Tables

What is the difference between a fire pit and a fire table?

Height and how you sit around it. A fire pit sits low, at roughly coffee-table height, so lounge seating gathers around it and people lean in. A fire table is built at dining or bar height, which means it doubles as a surface for glasses and plates and works with regular chairs or stools. The burner technology is often the same; what changes is how the space around it gets used.

Are gas fire pits better than wood-burning ones?

For most Southern California backyards, gas is the practical choice: it lights instantly, produces no embers or smoke to drift into a neighbour's yard, and leaves no ash to shovel out. Wood delivers the crackle and the smell that some people specifically want. Local rules on open burning and air quality vary by city, so it is worth checking what applies at your address before settling on wood.

Natural gas or propane for an outdoor fire pit?

Natural gas suits a permanent, frequently used feature — no tanks to swap, and it never runs out mid-evening — but it needs a line run to the location. Propane gives you flexibility where no line reaches, and freestanding fire tables are often built to hide a tank in the base. The choice affects burner sizing and the specifications your installer works from, so settle it before the burner is ordered.

How big should a fire pit be, and how far from seating?

Proportion to the seating area matters more than raw diameter: a fire feature that is too small disappears in a large patio, and one that is too large pushes seating back against the walls. Manufacturers publish minimum clearances to combustibles and to seating for each product, and those figures should drive the layout. We size the feature against your patio dimensions and furniture plan rather than a catalogue page.

Can a fire pit be built into an existing patio?

Often, yes. A built-in fire pit needs a stable base, a gas supply routed to it and proper venting in the enclosure. On an existing hardscape that usually means trenching for the gas line and building the enclosure on top, which your contractor can confirm on site. Where trenching is not practical, a freestanding gas fire table with a concealed tank gives a similar result with no excavation.

What does an outdoor fire pit cost?

The spread is wide because the product is a package, not a single item. A freestanding fire table is one purchase. A custom built-in fire feature adds the enclosure, the stone or tile veneer, the burner and ignition system, the gas work and the labour. Tell us which direction you are leaning and we can give you a realistic figure to plan against.

What maintenance does a gas fire pit need?

Light, on the whole. Keep the burner and its ports clear of debris, rinse and re-level the fire media occasionally, and cover the feature when it will sit unused through a wet stretch. Have the ignition and gas connections checked periodically, and follow the manufacturer's guidance for your specific product.

Do you supply fire pits to landscape contractors and designers?

Yes. We work with landscape contractors, hardscape installers and designers throughout Southern California — trade pricing, manufacturer specifications and rough-in information for plan sets, burner and ignition components matched to the enclosure, and deliveries coordinated with your build calendar.

Orange County. Coastal communities like Newport Beach, Corona del Mar, Laguna Beach and Huntington Beach sit in salt air year-round, which is worth weighing when choosing between powder-coated metal, concrete and porcelain tops. Inland and south-county neighbourhoods — Irvine, Coto de Caza, Ladera Ranch, Rancho Mission Viejo, Yorba Linda — tend toward larger patios where a dining-height table or a linear run along a seat wall does more than a single round pit.

Los Angeles County. South Bay and Peninsula properties in Manhattan Beach, Hermosa Beach, Redondo Beach, Palos Verdes and Rolling Hills often work with tighter footprints and strong exposure, where a compact fire table earns its space. Westside and coastal projects in Beverly Hills, Bel Air, Brentwood, Pacific Palisades and Malibu are frequently view-oriented, so the height of the feature matters as much as its size — a low pit keeps the horizon open in a way a tall one does not.

San Diego County. North coastal projects in Carlsbad, Encinitas, Solana Beach and Del Mar, and inland ones in Rancho Santa Fe, Fairbanks Ranch and Poway, cover everything from courtyard patios to large terraces. Whatever the layout and available utilities, we can identify products and specifications that suit the site and coordinate the finish with the hardscape around it.

Wind, overheads and exposure. Across all three counties the same two site conditions decide how well a fire feature performs: how exposed it is to prevailing wind, and what sits above it. Pergolas, shade sails and covered patios each carry clearance requirements, and a feature set below the top of a wall behaves very differently from one on an open terrace. We factor both in when specifying.
