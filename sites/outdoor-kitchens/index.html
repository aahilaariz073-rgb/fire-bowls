Why an Outdoor Kitchen

Stop Cooking Indoors While Everybody Else Is Outside

The problem with a barbecue on a patio is not the barbecue. It is that everything else — the plates, the drinks, the prep, the bin, the second pair of tongs — is still inside, so the cook spends the evening walking back and forth through a sliding door while the party happens without them.

An outdoor kitchen fixes that by moving the surrounding work outdoors, not just the flame. Counter space either side of the grill, cold storage within reach, a sink if you prep outside, and somewhere for people to sit while you cook. In a climate where Southern California backyards are usable most of the year, that is the difference between a grill you use on weekends and a room you use every week.

Design the workflow first and the appliance list follows. Pick the appliances first and you end up with a beautiful run that is awkward to cook in.

BellaFina Outdoors is an outdoor living design center, not a box shifter. We help homeowners and trade professionals work through layout, appliance selection, countertop material, ventilation and finish — and coordinate all of it with the stone, tile, fire features and shade structures going in around the kitchen.

Complete Outdoor Kitchens

A Full Outdoor Kitchen, Planned as One Run

A complete outdoor kitchen is a layout before it is a shopping list. The straight run is the simplest and suits a wall or a fence line. An L-shape separates cooking from prep and serving, which is what most people actually want once they think about where guests stand. A U-shape or an island with a bar overhang turns the kitchen into the place people gather rather than a station the cook works at alone.

Once the shape is settled, the appliances, the counter material and the utility runs all fall out of it — and the island can be framed to the right dimensions the first time.

  • Straight, L-shaped, U-shaped and island layouts sized to the space
  • Modular islands or fully custom-built runs, veneered to match the hardscape
  • Bar overhangs and seating heights worked out with the furniture in mind
  • Gas, water, drainage and electrical requirements identified for your contractor

Built-In BBQs & Grills

Built-In BBQs: The Piece Everything Else Is Built Around

The grill sets the dimensions of the island, the cut-out it drops into, the gas supply it needs and the clearances the surrounding structure has to respect. That is why it is worth choosing early — a grill swapped late in the build is an island rebuilt.

Size is decided by how many people you cook for at once rather than by how much room is available; burner count and cooking area matter more than overall width. Beyond the main grill, the same run can carry side burners for sauces and sides, a power burner for a wok or a stockpot, a smoker or kamado, and a rotisserie — each with its own cut-out and gas requirement.

  • Built-in gas grills across a range of sizes, burner counts and finishes
  • Natural gas or propane configurations, matched to your utility supply
  • Side burners, power burners, smokers, kamados and rotisserie options
  • Cut-out dimensions and clearance specifications for your installer

How to Plan an Outdoor Kitchen: What to Decide Before You Build

Five decisions shape how an outdoor kitchen works and what it costs — and every one of them is cheaper to settle on paper than in concrete.

Layout & Workflow
Decide where the cook stands and which way they face before anything else. A straight run is simple and fits a wall. An L-shape separates hot cooking from cold prep and gives guests somewhere to stand that is not in the way. A bar overhang turns the run into seating. Counter space either side of the grill is the single most common thing people wish they had added.
Modular or Custom
Modular islands arrive prefabricated and are assembled and finished on site — quicker, easier to price, ideal for a regular run. A custom-built island is framed in place, so it can follow an angled patio, wrap a corner or carry the same stone as the rest of the hardscape. Budget, timeline and how irregular the space is usually decide which route makes sense.
Appliance Selection
Choose the grill early, because its cut-out and gas requirement set the island dimensions. Then work outward: refrigeration and drawers earn their place in nearly every kitchen; a sink is worth it if you prep outdoors; side burners, smokers, pizza ovens and beverage centres are valuable to the people who use them and dead weight to everyone else. We'd rather ask how you cook than sell you a full suite.
Countertop Material
Outdoors, the criteria change: UV stability so the colour holds, how the surface behaves in direct sun, resistance to food and citrus staining, and coastal salt exposure. Granite, porcelain, quartzite, concrete and dense sintered surfaces all have a case; standard indoor quartz generally does not, because UV can discolour it. We can weigh the options against where your run sits.
Utilities, Ventilation & Clearances
Gas to the grill, water and drainage for a sink, power for refrigeration and lighting — all of it easier to run before the hardscape is finished. Any grill under a solid roof or covered patio generally needs a ventilation hood sized to the appliance, and every product publishes clearances to combustibles. These are design-stage items, not finish-stage ones.

Outdoor Kitchen Questions

Common Questions About Outdoor Kitchens & Built-In BBQs

What does a complete outdoor kitchen include?

At minimum: a built-in grill, a work surface either side of it, storage below and a way to get gas to it. Most complete kitchens add a sink with hot and cold water, a refrigerator, storage drawers and a bar or counter overhang for seating. From there it depends on how you cook — side burners, a pizza oven, a smoker or a kamado, a beverage centre, an ice maker, or a ventilation hood if the run sits under a solid roof.

How much does an outdoor kitchen cost?

It ranges widely, because an outdoor kitchen is a construction project rather than a single purchase. The variables are the length of the run, the appliances going into it, the countertop material, the veneer on the island, and the gas, water, electrical and drainage work behind it. A compact BBQ island with a grill and storage sits at one end; a long L-shaped kitchen with a full appliance suite and a stone counter sits well above it. Tell us roughly what you want to cook on and we can give you a realistic figure to plan against.

Should I buy a modular BBQ island or build a custom outdoor kitchen?

Modular islands arrive as prefabricated sections that get assembled and finished on site — faster to install, easier to price and a good fit for a straightforward run. A custom-built kitchen is framed and veneered in place, which lets it follow an angled patio, wrap a corner, match an unusual height or carry the same stone as the rest of the hardscape. Budget, timeline and how irregular the space is usually decide it.

What is the best countertop material for an outdoor kitchen?

The candidates are granite, porcelain, quartzite, concrete and dense sintered surfaces. Outdoors, the questions are different from indoors: UV stability so the colour holds, thermal behaviour in direct sun, staining from food and citrus, and how the material handles freeze or coastal salt air. Standard indoor quartz is generally not recommended outdoors because UV can discolour it. We can walk you through the options against where the run sits and how much sun it takes.

Does an outdoor kitchen need ventilation?

It depends on what is above it. A grill under open sky needs none. A grill under a solid roof, a covered patio or any enclosure generally requires a ventilation hood sized to the appliance, and the manufacturer publishes the requirement along with clearances to combustibles. This is a safety item, not a finish item — it should be settled at the design stage, before the island is framed.

Can an outdoor kitchen be built on an existing patio?

Usually. The work is in the utilities rather than the island: gas to the grill, water and drainage if there is a sink, and power for a refrigerator, lighting or ignition. On existing hardscape that generally means trenching and patching, which your contractor can assess on site. Where the utility runs are impractical, a shorter island with a propane grill and no plumbing gets much of the way there.

Which appliances are worth including, and which are not?

Work backwards from how you actually entertain. A refrigerator and storage drawers earn their place in almost every kitchen, because they stop the trips indoors that pull the cook away from the party. A sink is worth it if you prep outdoors rather than carrying platters out. Side burners, pizza ovens, smokers and beverage centres are genuinely useful to the people who use them and dead weight to everyone else — so we would rather ask how you cook than sell you a full suite.

Do you supply outdoor kitchen components to contractors and designers?

Yes. We work with landscape contractors, builders and designers throughout Southern California — trade pricing, appliance cut sheets and rough-in dimensions for your plan set, matched components across a single run, and deliveries coordinated with your build calendar so the appliances land when the island is ready for them.

Coastal projects in Newport Beach, Corona del Mar, Laguna Beach, Huntington Beach and Costa Mesa contend with salt air year-round, which matters for stainless grades, hardware and counter surfaces alike. Los Angeles County. South Bay and Peninsula properties in Manhattan Beach, Hermosa Beach, Redondo Beach, Palos Verdes and Rolling Hills often work within tight footprints, where a compact run with well-chosen appliances beats a long one that crowds the patio. Westside and coastal projects in Beverly Hills, Bel Air, Brentwood, Pacific Palisades and Malibu are frequently view-oriented and often covered, which makes ventilation and counter height part of the design conversation from the start.

San Diego County. North coastal projects in Carlsbad, Encinitas, Solana Beach and Del Mar, and inland ones in Rancho Santa Fe, Fairbanks Ranch, Poway and Coronado, span courtyard patios through to large terraces with room for a full kitchen and a separate bar. Whatever the layout and available utilities, we can identify components and specifications that suit the site.

Covered patios and shade structures. Across all three counties the single condition that changes an outdoor kitchen design most is what sits above it. An open-sky run has few constraints; a run under a solid roof, pergola or shade structure brings ventilation requirements and clearances to combustibles into play. We factor that in when specifying, rather than leaving it to be discovered at inspection.
