Why Fire Bowls

The Feature That Changes a Backyard After Dark

A pool is a daytime feature. It photographs beautifully at noon and gets used all summer — and then the sun goes down. Fire features extend the visual impact of the pool beyond daylight, adding warmth, movement and another architectural element to the outdoor environment.

Fire features have become a popular way to extend the enjoyment of Orange County outdoor spaces after dark. Fire reflects off water, so a single bowl reads as two. And unlike a fire pit set out on a lawn, a fire bowl becomes part of the pool's architecture — it sits on the structure that is already there.

The difference between a fire feature that looks designed and one that looks added on is decided before anything is ordered — in the bowl size, the placement, and the burner behind it.

BellaFina Outdoors is more than a place to purchase fire features. As an outdoor living design center, we help homeowners and trade professionals navigate size, material, finish, ignition and placement to select the right solution for the project. With showroom displays available for viewing, product expertise and access to leading fire-feature manufacturers, we make it easier to bring the entire outdoor space together.

Pool Fire Bowls

Outdoor Fire Bowls at the Water's Edge

Outdoor fire bowls do their best work at a pool, and they mount on the structure it already has: a raised bond beam, a spa wall, a pedestal or a pilaster. The classic placements are a matched pair flanking a raised spa, a run spaced evenly along a long wall, or a single larger bowl set off-center where a symmetrical pair would crowd the space.

Fire & Water Bowls

Fire and Water Features: Flame Above, Water Below

A fire and water bowl burns on top and spills into the pool below — either a full sheet off the lip or a scupper stream from the face. It is the feature people picture when they picture a modern pool, and the reason is acoustic as much as visual: moving water covers street noise, and a flame above it gives the sound somewhere to look.

The same family includes water-only bowls — spillway bowls, scuppers and wall spouts. Running fire on some positions and water on others, matched in finish, keeps a run reading as one design while leaving room to add fire later.

  • Full spillway lips or scupper spouts, in matched finishes
  • Water-only bowls to balance a run now and convert later
  • Spill height, plumbing and auto-fill coordinated with your builder
  • Finishes coordinated with your waterline tile and coping

How to Choose a Fire Bowl: What to Decide Before You Order

Five decisions shape how a fire feature looks and how it fits the project — and all five are easier to settle before the concrete is poured.

Material & Finish
Copper naturally develops a distinctive patina over time, creating a finish that continues to evolve with exposure to the elements. Powder-coated stainless steel and GFRC concrete offer different looks and different maintenance considerations, and GFRC can be matched to coping so the bowl reads as part of the structure. Which suits your project depends on the setting and the finish you are after — something we can help you weigh in the showroom.
Diameter & Height
Proportion against the pilaster matters more than absolute size. Smaller bowls tend to suit spa corners and tighter decks; a larger bowl earns its place as a single focal point or along a long wall. We size it against your plan rather than a catalogue page.
Fuel Type
Natural gas can be convenient for a permanent feature used frequently, with no tanks to refill; propane can offer flexibility where no line reaches the pool. The choice affects burner configuration and the specifications your installer will work from, so it is worth settling before the burner is ordered.
Ignition & Control
Match-lit offers a straightforward ignition option, while electronic ignition provides added convenience and control. Depending on the product, automated systems can integrate with pool controls so fire, water and lighting are managed together. It is worth matching the ignition to how often you expect to use the feature.
Fire or Fire & Water
A fire-only bowl needs a structure and a fuel supply. A fire and water bowl also involves plumbing and a spill height set relative to the waterline, which your builder or installer will determine on site. Either way it is worth deciding early, while the plumbing is still accessible.

Fire Bowl Questions

Common Questions About Fire & Water Features

What is the difference between a fire bowl and a fire and water bowl?

A fire bowl holds a burner and fire media, producing flame only, and can be placed anywhere with a fuel supply. A fire and water bowl adds a plumbed water supply that spills from the lip or a scupper spout into the pool below, combining flame, moving water and the sound of a spill in one feature.

How many fire bowls does a pool need?

Many pool designs use fire bowls in pairs or symmetrical configurations — flanking a raised spa, bracketing a baja shelf, or spaced along a raised bond beam — while others call for a single statement feature. The right number depends on the scale, layout and overall design of the pool, which is why we look at the plan before suggesting a configuration.

Can fire bowls be added to an existing pool?

Often, yes. It generally takes a structure to set the bowl on — a raised wall, pilaster or pedestal — plus a fuel supply routed to it, and a water line if you want a spillway. Retrofits are common; your contractor can confirm what the existing structure and utilities will support.

Natural gas or propane for an outdoor fire feature?

Natural gas can be a convenient choice for permanent fire features that will be used frequently, with no tanks to refill, while propane can provide flexibility depending on the project and available utilities. The choice affects burner configuration and the specifications your installer works from, so it is worth settling before the burner is ordered.

Which materials hold up best near a pool and the coast?

Copper naturally develops a distinctive patina over time, a finish that keeps evolving with exposure to the elements. Powder-coated stainless steel and GFRC concrete offer different appearances and maintenance considerations. Coastal exposure and pool chemistry both factor in, and we can help you weigh the options for your setting.

What does a pool fire bowl cost?

Budget is shaped by material, size, ignition and whether water is involved, rather than by the bowl alone — burner, fire media, ignition and gas work all add to the total. We quote the package, so tell us about the pool and we can give you a realistic figure to plan against.

How do fire bowls handle wind, and what maintenance do they need?

Exposure varies by site, and burner selection and placement both play a part — a bowl set below the top of a wall is generally less exposed than one above it. Maintenance is typically light: keep the fire media clear, rinse the finish periodically, and have the ignition checked when the pool is serviced. Always follow the manufacturer's guidance for your product.

Do you work with pool builders and landscape contractors?

Yes. We work with pool builders, landscape contractors and designers throughout Orange County — trade pricing, manufacturer specifications and rough-in information for plan sets, access to compatible burner and ignition components, and deliveries coordinated with your build calendar.
