Fire & Water Features

Fire and Water Bowls: Two Elements, One Feature

Fire and water bowls burn on top and spill into the pool below — a sheet of water off the lip, or a scupper stream from the face. The sound of moving water plus a live flame does more for a backyard than either element does alone.

The same family covers water-only options: spillway bowls, scuppers and wall spouts that can be added now and converted or paired with fire later as the project budget allows.

  • Fire-on-water bowls with full spillway or scupper spouts
  • Matching water-only bowls for balanced runs along a wall
  • Auto-fill, plumbing and return details planned with your builder
  • Finishes that coordinate with your tile, coping and stone

What to Decide Before You Order

The five choices that determine how a fire bowl looks, installs and performs.

Material & Finish
Copper for a living patina, powder-coated stainless for coastal durability, GFRC concrete and cast stone for a monolithic modern look that matches coping and pilasters.
Diameter & Height
Bowl diameter should read in proportion to the pilaster or wall it sits on. Smaller accent bowls suit spa corners and tight decks; larger bowls carry a long pool wall or a single focal placement.
Fuel Type
Natural gas for a permanent, always-on feature with a run to the pad; propane where no gas line exists. Fuel choice drives burner orifice, line sizing and BTU planning.
Ignition & Control
Match-lit is the simplest and least expensive; push-button and electronic ignition add convenience; automated systems tie into pool controls so fire, water and lighting run from one app.
Fire or Fire & Water
Fire-only bowls sit on any structure. Fire and water bowls need a plumbed supply and a spill point set at the right height above the waterline for a clean sheet or scupper stream.

Fire Bowl Questions

Common Questions About Fire & Water Features

What is the difference between a fire bowl and a fire and water bowl?

A fire bowl holds a burner and media only — it produces flame and nothing else, and can be placed anywhere with a fuel supply. A fire and water bowl adds a plumbed water supply that spills from the lip or a scupper spout into the pool below, so you get flame, moving water and the sound of a spill from a single feature.

How many fire bowls does a pool need?

Most residential pools use two or four bowls placed symmetrically — flanking a raised spa, bracketing a baja shelf, or spaced evenly along a raised bond beam. A single larger bowl works well as an off-center focal point on a smaller pool. Placement and spacing matter more than count, which is why we look at the plan before recommending quantity.

Can fire bowls be added to an existing pool?

Often, yes. The requirements are a structure to set the bowl on — a raised wall, pilaster or pedestal — plus a fuel supply routed to it, and a water line if you want a spillway. Retrofits are very common; the practical questions are how the gas run is routed and whether the existing structure can carry the bowl.

Natural gas or propane for an outdoor fire feature?

Natural gas is the better choice for a permanent feature you'll use often: no tanks to refill, and consistent output. Propane makes sense when there's no gas line at the pool and running one isn't practical. The fuel decision affects burner orifice, BTU output and line sizing, so it should be settled before the burner is ordered.

Which materials hold up best near a pool and the coast?

Copper develops a patina over time and is a favorite for warm, traditional looks. Powder-coated stainless steel handles salt air and pool chemistry well. GFRC concrete and cast stone give a modern, monolithic look that blends with coping and pilasters. In coastal Huntington Beach conditions we generally steer clients toward stainless or GFRC unless a copper patina is the goal.

Do you work with pool builders and landscape contractors?

Yes. We supply pool builders, landscape contractors and designers throughout Orange County with trade pricing, spec sheets and rough-in details for plan sets, stocked burner and ignition components, and deliveries scheduled around the build calendar.
